\section{Overview}

Plot 1 (plot1\_proxy\_landscape.pdf) consists of three panels arranged in a $2 \times 2$ grid: a proxy landscape scatter (left, spanning both rows) and two cost-comparison bar charts (right, stacked). The figure compares the computational cost of two evaluation pathways:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Proxy evaluation}: Graph-theoretic metrics (TE, ORC) computed on candidate architectures without training.
  \item \textbf{Full evaluation}: Train the neural network and evaluate robustness via perturbation sweep.
\end{enumerate}

This document provides the analytical justification for the default FLOPS estimates used in the plot:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Proxy: $\sim 10^7$ FLOP per architecture
  \item Full: $\sim 10^{12}$ FLOP per (model, seed)
\end{itemize}

These are \emph{order-of-magnitude} estimates, not measured values. The ratio ($\sim 10^5$) illustrates that proxy evaluation is orders of magnitude cheaper than full training and robustness evaluation.

\section{Proxy Evaluation Complexity}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The proxy evaluation is implemented in \texttt{architecture\_refinement.metrics\_te\_orc.compute\_paper\_3\_proxies(G)}. It computes two metrics on a Watts--Strogatz--Flex graph $G$ with $H = 32$ nodes and $E \approx H \cdot k$ edges (where $k \in \{2, 4, \ldots, 24\}$; typical $k \approx 12$ yields $E \approx 400$).

\subsection{Degree Entropy (TE)}

The topological entropy proxy uses the Shannon entropy of the empirical degree distribution:
\begin{equation}
  H_{\text{deg}}(G) = -\sum_k p(k) \log p(k), \quad p(k) = \frac{|\{i : \deg(i) = k\}|}{N}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Operations:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Degree computation: $O(N) = O(32)$
  \item \texttt{np.unique} for histogram: $O(N \log N) \approx 32 \cdot 5 \approx 160$
  \item Entropy sum: $O(\text{unique degrees}) \leq O(N)$, typically $\sim 10$ terms
  \item Each term: 1 division, 1 $\log$, 1 multiply, 1 add
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Total:} $\sim 10^3$--$10^4$ FLOP. Negligible compared to ORC.

\subsection{Ollivier--Ricci Curvature (ORC)}

The ORC proxy computes the signed mean Ollivier--Ricci curvature over edges:
\begin{equation}
  \kappa(u,v) = 1 - \frac{W_1(m_u, m_v)}{d(u,v)}, \qquad
  \text{ORC}(G) = \frac{1}{|E|} \sum_{(u,v) \in E} \kappa(u,v)
\end{equation}
where $m_u$, $m_v$ are neighbor measures (mass $\alpha$ at node, $(1-\alpha)$ uniform over 1-hop neighbors), and $W_1$ is the Wasserstein-1 (Earth Mover's) distance.

\textbf{Implementation steps (per graph):}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{All-pairs shortest path lengths:} \texttt{nx.all\_pairs\_shortest\_path\_length(G)}.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item For each of $N = 32$ nodes: BFS or Dijkstra over $E \approx 400$ edges.
    \item Per node: $O(N + E) \approx 432$; total $O(N(N+E)) \approx 32 \cdot 432 \approx 1.4 \times 10^4$.
  \end{itemize}

  \item \textbf{Per edge} $(u,v) \in E$ (up to $E \approx 400$ edges):
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{\_support\_and\_measure}: Build discrete measure on support of size $|\text{supp}| \leq \deg + 1 \approx 13$.
    \item Cost matrix $M \in \mathbb{R}^{|\text{supp}_u| \times |\text{supp}_v|}$: fill $O(|\text{supp}|^2) \approx 169$ entries using graph distances.
    \item \textbf{Wasserstein-1}: Either
    \begin{itemize}
      \item POT \texttt{emd2}: $O(n^3)$ for $n = |\text{supp}| \approx 10$ $\Rightarrow$ $\sim 10^3$ per edge, or
      \item LP (SciPy HiGHS): $m \cdot n$ variables ($\approx 100$), equality constraints; typically $O(n^3)$ to $O(n^4)$ iterations $\Rightarrow$ $\sim 10^4$--$10^5$ per edge.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Aggregate ORC FLOP:}
\begin{align}
  \text{ORC}_{\text{FLOP}} &\approx \underbrace{1.4 \times 10^4}_{\text{shortest paths}} + \underbrace{E \cdot (10^4 \text{--} 10^5)}_{\text{per-edge W1}} \\
  &\approx 1.4 \times 10^4 + 400 \times (2 \times 10^4) \approx 8 \times 10^6
\end{align}

Using the LP fallback (slower path): $400 \times 10^5 = 4 \times 10^7$. With POT (faster): $400 \times 10^4 = 4 \times 10^6$.

\textbf{Conclusion:} Proxy FLOP is dominated by ORC. A conservative order-of-magnitude estimate is
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Proxy FLOP} \approx 10^7}
\end{equation}

\section{Full Evaluation Complexity}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Full evaluation consists of (1) training the neural network and (2) robustness evaluation via perturbation sweep.

\subsection{Model and Training Configuration}

The Paper 3 pipeline uses CfC (Closed-form Continuous) / NCP-style models with sparse wiring. Typical configuration:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Hidden size $H = 32$, sparse connectivity $E_{\text{active}} \approx 400$--$800$
  \item Parameter count: $\sim 4{,}000$--$15{,}000$ (from BranchedWiredCfC analysis)
  \item Training: CrossSession on BNCI2014\_001; $\sim 100$ epochs, $\sim 50$ batches/epoch
  \item Batch size: $\sim 32$; sequence length (after CNN front-end): $\sim 500$--$1000$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training FLOP}

Per batch:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Forward pass: Each timestep involves recurrent updates. Approximate FLOP per forward:
  \begin{equation}
    \text{FLOP}_{\text{fwd}} \approx \text{params} \times \text{batch} \times \text{seq} \times 2 \quad \text{(multiply-add)}
  \end{equation}
  With params $\approx 10^4$, batch $= 32$, seq $= 1000$:
  \begin{equation}
    \text{FLOP}_{\text{fwd}} \approx 10^4 \times 32 \times 1000 \times 2 \approx 6.4 \times 10^8
  \end{equation}
  \item Backward pass: $\approx 2 \times \text{FLOP}_{\text{fwd}}$ (gradient computation)
  \item Total per batch: $\approx 2 \times 10^9$
\end{itemize}

Total training:
\begin{equation}
  \text{FLOP}_{\text{train}} \approx 100 \times 50 \times (2 \times 10^9) \approx 10^{13}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Robustness Evaluation FLOP}

After training, the pipeline sweeps AR(1) drift intensities (e.g., 20 levels) over subjects/sessions. Each evaluation is a forward pass (no backward):
\begin{equation}
  \text{FLOP}_{\text{robust}} \approx 20 \times 9 \times (6.4 \times 10^8) \approx 1.2 \times 10^{11}
\end{equation}
(20 intensities, $\sim 9$ subjects/sessions, $\sim 6.4 \times 10^8$ FLOP per forward.)

\subsection{Total Full Evaluation}

\begin{equation}
  \text{FLOP}_{\text{full}} = \text{FLOP}_{\text{train}} + \text{FLOP}_{\text{robust}} \approx 10^{13} + 10^{11} \approx 10^{13}
\end{equation}

A conservative default for the plot (to avoid overstating the ratio) uses:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\text{Full FLOP} \approx 10^{12}}
\end{equation}
This is a lower bound; $10^{13}$ is more representative of typical runs.

\subsection{Ratio}

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\text{Full FLOP}}{\text{Proxy FLOP}} \approx \frac{10^{12}}{10^7} = 10^5
\end{equation}

The proxy is approximately \textbf{five orders of magnitude} cheaper than full evaluation, justifying its use for large-scale architecture search.

\section{Caveats and Overrides}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{itemize}
  \item These are \emph{analytical} estimates, not profiled FLOPS. Actual values depend on hardware, implementation (e.g., POT vs LP for W1), and exact model/training config.
  \item Override defaults via \texttt{experiment1\_summary.json}: \texttt{mean\_proxy\_flops\_est}, \texttt{mean\_full\_flops\_est}.
  \item Or pass \texttt{mean\_proxy\_flops} and \texttt{mean\_full\_flops} to \texttt{plot1()} when generating the figure.
\end{itemize}
