9. CONCLUSIONS AND IMPLICATIONS

HYDRA demonstrates strong and competitive performance across benchmark EEG datasets,
with several notable strengths that position it as a viable alternative to established
baselines. The model achieves robust baseline performance (0.75-0.85 ROC-AUC) that
consistently exceeds CNN-NCP and approaches EEGNet's performance in several settings.
Particularly impressive is HYDRA's robustness metrics, where it achieves AUPC values
(0.585-0.720) that rank second to EEGNet across datasets, with especially strong
performance for gaussian and eog noise types. In BI2015a, HYDRA's AUPC of 0.720 is
within 2% of EEGNet's 0.732, while significantly exceeding CNN-NCP's 0.577 by 25%.
This robust performance is complemented by HYDRA's exceptional response to
hyperparameter tuning in the SSVEP paradigm, where it shows dramatic improvements
(+18-25%) and reaches performance levels (0.84-0.96) and AUPC values (0.95+) that
are competitive with the best-performing models, ranking second only to EEGNet.

The most striking finding is HYDRA's exceptional robustness in the SSVEP CrossSubject
setting, where it demonstrates near-perfect performance under perturbation. HYDRA
achieves essentially zero degradation (0.04% for gaussian) at maximum perturbation
intensity, representing a 13-fold improvement over EEGNet and 7-fold improvement
over CNN-NCP for dropout noise. This exceptional robustness, combined with HYDRA's
strong tuned AUPC values (0.9475 for gaussian, 0.8567 for dropout) that are within
3% of or exceed EEGNet's performance, suggests that HYDRA may be particularly well-suited
for SSVEP applications where signal quality may vary. The model's strong gaussian noise
AUPC (0.7995 in BI2015a) also indicates robust handling of continuous noise artifacts,
which are common in real-world EEG recordings. HYDRA's appearance in the top 10 best
AUPC performances (ranking 3rd for SSVEP gaussian tuned and 10th for SSVEP dropout tuned)
further validates its competitive standing.

When compared to the other evaluated models, HYDRA occupies a compelling position in
the performance landscape. It consistently outperforms REEGNet in clean performance
metrics. HYDRA is generally competitive with
or exceeds CNN-NCP across most metrics, particularly in robustness (AUPC 0.720 vs
0.577 in BI2015a) and in tuned SSVEP configurations. Against EEGNet, HYDRA shows
stronger competition than initially apparent: while EEGNet maintains the highest clean
performance, HYDRA's robustness metrics are very close (AUPC within 2% in BI2015a,
within 1% in BNCI2014_001), and HYDRA actually shows better robustness for certain
noise types (eog in BI2015a, dropout in SSVEP tuned). The evaluation across three
diverse EEG paradigms reveals that HYDRA's performance is highly competitive, with
particular strengths in robustness metrics and SSVEP applications. The model's
combination of strong baseline performance, competitive robustness, and exceptional
SSVEP tuning benefits suggests it represents a valuable addition to the EEG
classification toolkit, offering a balance between accuracy and robustness that may
be preferable to CNN-NCP while providing tuning potential that EEGNet lacks.
